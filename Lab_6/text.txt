Minecraft is a sandbox video game where players explore vast, blocky worlds made of different materials such as stone, dirt, wood, and ores. Every world is procedurally generated, so terrain, caves, oceans, and villages appear in new layouts each time a player starts a game. From the first moments, players gather basic resources, craft tools, and build shelters to survive the dangers that appear at night.​ The game offers several core modes that change how it feels to play. In Survival mode, players manage health and hunger while mining, farming, and crafting better equipment to defend themselves from hostile mobs like zombies, skeletons, and creepers. In Creative mode, all blocks and items are unlimited, players can fly, and there is no damage, making it ideal for large builds, redstone experiments, and complex architectural projects. Adventure and Spectator modes focus on map-based challenges and free camera exploration, while Hardcore is a more punishing variant of Survival with only one life in certain editions.​ As players progress, they can travel to other dimensions beyond the familiar Overworld. The Nether is a dangerous, lava-filled realm with unique materials that are used for advanced crafting and transportation systems. The End dimension features mysterious structures and the Ender Dragon boss, giving players a long-term objective and a sense of completion once it is defeated.​ Multiplayer adds another layer to the experience by allowing many players to share the same world. Public and private servers often have custom rules, mini-games, and economies that encourage cooperation, trading, or competitive play. Modded servers and community-made plugins introduce new biomes, mobs, technologies, and magic systems that significantly expand what players can do beyond the base game.​ Creative players use Minecraft as a platform for designing cities, re-creating real-world landmarks, prototyping redstone machines, or telling interactive stories through custom maps. Educators and students also use the game to explore topics such as programming logic, architecture, and environmental science in a more engaging way. Because of this flexibility, Minecraft supports casual building sessions, long survival campaigns, and structured learning projects, all within the same simple block-based world.
